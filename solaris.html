01 / The challenge

How do you keep growing when you’re already a market leader?

Solaris became one of Russia’s best-selling cars shortly after its launch in 2011. But maintaining that position required continuous work. Customer expectations were evolving, competitors were introducing new models and technologies, and Hyundai needed to keep its offering relevant without losing its price advantage.

My role was to lead the research behind these decisions — understanding what customers valued, which features and configurations they were willing to pay for, and how Solaris could continue to stand out while supporting long-term loyalty to Hyundai.

03 / The insight

What customers valued and what they would pay for

Research identified a clear pattern: practical features that improved comfort and safety in Russian driving conditions generated more interest than cosmetic upgrades or technologies whose benefits were unclear.

Heated windshieldHighly valued in long winters
Practical visibilitySafety and functionality mattered more than decorative lighting
Keyless startNovelty alone did not justify extra cost
Parking assistanceClear everyday value for city drivers
“The opportunity wasn't to add everything. It was to identify the features that made customers feel the car was built for them.”

04 / From research to recommendations

How the findings informed product decisions

FEATURES

Prioritise useful innovation

Emphasise heating, safety and parking-assistance features as differentiators; avoid relying on decorative upgrades as a core USP.

PRICING

Test perceived value

Assess willingness to pay for individual features and packages, recognising that interest does not automatically translate into purchase intent.

CUSTOMER JOURNEY

Simplify the choice

Customers liked the idea of customisation but struggled with crowded trim lists. Recommend clearer grouping, comparison and explanation of options.

05 / Business context

How Solaris performed over time

Solaris was already a strong seller before the 2014 clinic. Continuous product research formed part of a broader company effort to maintain relevance and differentiate the model. In 2016, Solaris reached the top position in Russia's model sales ranking.

2011

Solaris launches in Russia.

2014

One documented research wave tests the refreshed model, features, packages, price expectations and communications.

2015

Sales reach approximately 115,868 units.*

2016

Solaris becomes Russia's best-selling car model; Hyundai launches Creta, expanding its portfolio.

*Historical model sales figures are contextual market outcomes, not directly attributable to one research project. Figures and ranking should be checked against original AEB annual reports before external publication. The extent of Solaris–Creta cannibalisation has not been quantified here.

06 / Beyond one model

Looking beyond Solaris to the wider Hyundai portfolio

My remit extended beyond Solaris to research supporting the wider Hyundai model range, including Creta. This provided a broader view of customer segments, competitive positioning and the trade-offs between individual model performance and portfolio growth.

The key lesson: a single model's sales are only one part of the story. Good product strategy considers how different propositions serve distinct needs and contribute to the total portfolio.

The takeaway

What this work means for my approach to product growth
