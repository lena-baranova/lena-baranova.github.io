Launching a premium brand
without a premium legacy

How qualitative research with premium car buyers and experts uncovered a new luxury audience and informed positioning, pricing and go-to-market recommendations for the Genesis standalone brand launch in Russia.

Russian market entry Pre-launch research Premium automotive
01 / Situation
Genesis was entering Russia as a standalone premium brand, competing for buyers of the premium segment accustomed to established European and Japanese names. Hyundai was known for quality, reliability and value — but those associations didn't automatically translate into premium credibility.
Established luxury
Heritage · Recognition · Status conventions
The Genesis challenge
New name · Unproven premium credibility · Product-led differentiation
02 / Task
Four questions the research needed to answer to inform product positioning, messaging and go-to-market decisions.
01 / Audience
Who would consider a new premium brand — and what unmet needs did they have?
02 / Positioning
How should Genesis, G80 and G90 be framed against established competitors?
03 / Pricing
What price point could feel credible and competitive for each model?
04 / Go-to-market
Which channels and associations could build awareness and premium legitimacy?
03 / Research
I conducted qualitative research and competitive analysis using pre-production Genesis vehicles alongside competitor cars — putting the cars in front of the people who would judge them, and going beyond surface reactions to understand perceptions, motivations and purchase barriers. The goal was not research for its own sake, but to build the client segmentation and insights needed to define product positioning and go-to-market strategy.
Existing premium owners
Established expectations and switching barriers
Potential upgraders
Owners considering a move into the luxury segment
Automotive experts

Research themes: Design & comfort · Brand perceptions · Status & social acceptance · Purchase motivations · Willingness to pay · Competitive alternatives
The breakthrough insight
Not every premium buyer wanted
the same kind of status.
The research identified a promising audience beyond traditional buyers of the premium segment: younger entrepreneurs, startup founders and C-level executives — internationally minded, open to new brands, and less constrained by conventional status symbols.
Traditional premium codes
Heritage · Recognition · Established status badges
Genesis opportunity
Contemporary design · Individuality · Openness to new brands
Qualitative positioning insight — not a quantified market segmentation.
From insight to strategy
Research translated into three areas of strategic recommendation.
01 / Positioning & messaging
Defined positioning for G80 and G90 targeting younger, open-minded premium buyers. Recommended messaging built on modern luxury and individuality rather than heritage.
02 / Model pricing
Developed price ranges for G80 and G90 based on competitor evaluation and perceived value. Specific figures are confidential.
03 / Go-to-market channels
Recommended complementing conventional media with selective sponsorships and high-profile business events to build credibility with entrepreneurial and executive audiences.
Customer & expert research → Audience opportunity → Positioning & pricing → GTM recommendations
04 / Results
Audience opportunity defined
Identified internationally minded premium buyers receptive to an alternative luxury proposition.
Pricing recommendations established
Delivered competitive price-positioning guidance for G80 and G90.
Product and marketing informed
Shared recommendations on positioning, purchase barriers and credibility-building channels.

Genesis launched in Russia in 2016 with G90, followed by G80 in 2017. The brand subsequently appeared in premium business settings — the type of association highlighted in the GTM recommendations.
